# Buenos Aires

> Roadside hitching spots go stale. For current places to hitch in and out of this city, use [maps.hitchwiki.org](https://maps.hitchwiki.org/) and the live Hitchwiki page linked at the end of this chapter. This book keeps general orientation, not a pin gazetteer.

\

**Buenos Aires** is the capital and largest city of [Argentina](Argentina "Argentina"){.wikilink}.

The city itself, with around 3 million inhabitants, is also known as *Capital Federal*, and is sometimes referred to a CABA, the acronym for its full official name *Ciudad Autónoma de Buenoes Aires*, which is more than a municipal government but not quite the same as a province. Surrounding it to the north, west, and south are the *partidos* in the province of Buenos Aires, in which live the majority of the metro\'s population. Buenos Aires refers to both city and province, though something or one from the city is *porteño*, while from the province is *bonarense*. The terms *Gran Buenos Aires* (also GBA) and *conurbano* are used to differentiate when referring to the periphery or the centre (*Capital Federal*, CABA). While the urban fabric is unbroken, the border between the two is clearly demarcated by Avenida General Paz, a 6-12 lane highway (counting frontage roads) to the north and west, and the Matanza River (there referred to as the Riachuelo) to the south.

CABA is very densely built and populated, and (with a few exceptions detailed below) you will without question have better luck hitchhiking on some far edge of the *conurbano*.

### Any direction. General hitchhiking points {#any_direction._general_hitchhiking_points}

There are places in Buenos Aires where many trucks pass by or stop. You could go to the road that goes around Retiro (or the Puerto Madero area). On the other side of the park, in front of the train station, there is a crowded intersection. Trucks leave from there to all directions. Or from the Mercado Central. Most trucks from there go north, since fruits come from the north.

## The ferry to [Uruguay](Uruguay "Uruguay"){.wikilink} {#the_ferry_to_uruguay}

The easiest way to get from Buenos Aires to Uruguay is by ferry to [Colonia del Sacramento](Colonia_del_Sacramento "Colonia del Sacramento"){.wikilink}. From the port in Puerto Madero/Retiro leave the main ferries by the companies Seacat Colonia, Colonia Express and Buquebus. Seacat Colonia is part of Buquebus and is usually the cheapest. Booking on their website is the easiest way to get a ticket. Check their website in both the Argentinian and the Uruguayan version. Found out that booking from the Uruguayan site was 50% cheaper: US\$20 instead of USD\$40 one way. If you don\'t have a credit card, ask an Argentinian to buy it for you and pay them back in cash.

There\'s also a ferry leaving from Tigre to Carmelo in Uruguay that apparently goes via Isla Martín Garcia, an Argentinian island that\'s actually closer to Uruguay in the Rio de la Plata.
