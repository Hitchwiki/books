# Long distance hitchhiking

**Long distance hitchhiking** can be a lot of fun as well as exhausting. You should probably not embark on a long distance hitching trip if you haven\'t hitchhiked at least a couple of times before, especially if you\'re planning to go on your own. If you\'re with someone else who has plenty of experience: go for it.

With long distance hitchhikers probably mean more than 1 day of traveling. 1000 km in [Western Europe](Western_Europe "Western Europe"){.wikilink} doesn\'t really count as you can probably arrive at night if you\'re leaving early morning. More trips like from [Amsterdam](Amsterdam "Amsterdam"){.wikilink} to [Istanbul](Istanbul "Istanbul"){.wikilink} or something.

There are several ways of doing it:

1.  As fast as possible - in which case you probably want to bring a tent to sleep somewhere close to the road or don\'t sleep at all - this is very well possible by jumping from service station to service station, under the midnight sun or if you\'re slightly crazy and equipped with a reflecting vest.
2.  As convenient as possible - in which case you want to contact friends on the way or find places to stay with through [hospitality exchange](finding_accommodation "hospitality exchange"){.wikilink} - or if you have a budget: get a hotel (which can be quite cheap in countries like [Syria](Syria "Syria"){.wikilink} or [China](China "China"){.wikilink}).

Some more tips before leaving:

- check online maps (e.g. [Google Maps](http://maps.google.com/)) for which way traffic goes
- make sure you have some [food](food "food"){.wikilink} to start with, at least for the first days
- if you plan on [hitchhiking at night](hitchhiking_at_night "hitchhiking at night"){.wikilink}, be prepared - and don\'t do this if it doesn\'t feel safe
