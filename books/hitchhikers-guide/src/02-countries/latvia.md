# Latvia

**Latvia** is a Baltic country. It is a member state of the [European Union](European_Union "European Union"){.wikilink} as well as the [Schengen Agreement](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"){.wikilink}. It borders [Estonia](Estonia "Estonia"){.wikilink}, [Lithuania](Lithuania "Lithuania"){.wikilink}, [Russia](Russia "Russia"){.wikilink} and [Belarus](Belarus "Belarus"){.wikilink}.

### Legality

Latvian law says nothing about hitchhiking, so on ordinary roads it is legal --- and Latvia has no *automaģistrāle* category at all, only the *ātrgaitas ceļš*. [Paragraph 159.¹ of the *Ceļu satiksmes noteikumi*](https://likumi.lv/ta/id/274865-celu-satiksmes-noteikumi), a chapter added in January 2024, forbids the use of an *ātrgaitas ceļš* --- crossing it included --- to pedestrians, to animals outside a vehicle, and to cyclists, e-scooters, mopeds, tractors, carts and anything unable to exceed 60 km/h. Paragraph 159.³ bans stopping and standing on it, together with U-turns and reversing, so no driver can lawfully pull over for you. A forced stop belongs on the *nomale* or a designated stopping place under 159.², and that is also where asking makes sense. Everywhere else --- the A and P roads that carry most Latvian traffic --- hitchhiking is unregulated, so stand on the shoulder and thumb.

It is allowed to hitchhike in Latvia (at least there are no laws which forbid that), but of course in places where you do not danger road safety. It is a common practice here to use signs of the cities or countries where you want to get a ride to, but thumb also works fine.

### Language

The Latvian population is divided into Latvian speakers and Russian speakers. Most of adult Latvians will know both languages. If you know some Russian, feel free to use it here. Most of young people will speak English as well. If they don\'t, they will at least try to communicate in it if they see you are a foreigner who needs help. Some people might speak German as well.

To catch some phrases you can also check [Eastern Europe phrasebook](Eastern_Europe_phrasebook "Eastern Europe phrasebook"){.wikilink}.

### Roads

Roads are getting better but don\'t expect European standards, especially in winter and early spring when temperature fluctuations destroy asphalt pretty easily. Be careful on dirt roads in countryside regions. Not only you get dust in your face from vehicles, but sometimes you can be hit by stones. On the other hand, traveling those roads usually offer the most beautiful and authentic scenery.

### Useful things {#useful_things}

Free internet is available in public and university libraries. Also almost all of Latvian cities and even villages will have free internet in the town\'s center - it is now a prestige thing.

There exist Facebook groups for ridesharing where you can often get a ride from Riga to other parts of Latvia for little money.. Group names are in form \"Riga - (city/town name) - Riga\". Use Facebook search to find them. For example, for Liepaja direction use <https://www.facebook.com/groups/840988475937147/> These groups are mostly used by young people and they are very likely to speak good English. <http://www.blablacar.com> is also popular.

Weather in Latvia can change pretty fast so don\'t lose hope if rain comes. Hide and wait. Sometimes it\'s over in 15-20 minutes.

## Hitchhiking

There are a lot of young people just hitchhiking around the country, to their hometowns, festivals, etc., especially during summer, so you\'ll probably meet someone on the road as well. People are used to hitchhikers and it is fairly easy to get a lift, whether along important routes such as the [E67](E67 "E67"){.wikilink} (*Via Baltica*) or in the middle of nowhere.

Good and easy hitchhiking experience. You rarely wait a long time and you have a big chance to pass by Latvia, or get into the capital by one or two cars. I did nearly 200km in one drive with a guy going from Lithuania to Riga. Afterwards took A3 to Tartu (est). Because there are a lot of people going directly to Tallinn from Riga you have a good chances make all that trip in one car. I was going to Tartu, so it was harder but anyway I was there in the same day. The majority of drivers spoke English.
