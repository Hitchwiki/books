# Finland

**Finland** is a part of the Northern [Europe](Europe "Europe"){.wikilink} and one of the Nordic countries. It is a member state of the [European Union](European_Union "European Union"){.wikilink} as well as the [Schengen Agreement](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"){.wikilink}.

## Hitchhiking

Its neighbouring countries are [Sweden](Sweden "Sweden"){.wikilink}, [Norway](Norway "Norway"){.wikilink} and [Russia](Russia "Russia"){.wikilink}, but the most probable transit country on your way hitchhiking there from Southern Europe is [Estonia](Estonia "Estonia"){.wikilink} as the [Via Baltica](E67 "Via Baltica"){.wikilink} ([E67](E67 "E67"){.wikilink}) road connects Finland with the Baltic States and [Poland](Poland "Poland"){.wikilink}.

Hitchhiking is fairly common in Finland you can regularly spot people hitchhiking to travel, some also hitchhike as a freetime activity.

In the south, it is a good idea to avoid motorways and opt for smaller local roads. Though there is less traffic, there is more room for cars to stop. Finnish drivers will only stop where they feel it is safe, so try to thumb at bus stops.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Finnish law says nothing about hitchhiking (*liftaus*), so it is legal wherever a pedestrian may lawfully be. [Section 58 of the *tieliikennelaki* (729/2018)](https://www.finlex.fi/fi/laki/ajantasa/2018/20180729) allows only motor-vehicle traffic on a *moottoritie* or *moottoriliikennetie*, which keeps you off the carriageway, the shoulder and the slip road. Section 37 forbids stopping on those roads anywhere except a signposted parking place, *palvelualue* or *levähdysalue* --- and, usefully, at a signposted bus stop, for the purpose of picking up or setting down a passenger. Service areas and rest areas are therefore both where you ask drivers and the only places they may lawfully stop for you. On every other road hitchhiking is unregulated: stand on the verge, facing the traffic.

It is illegal to hitchhike directly on motorways (*moottoritie*) and some two-land expressways roads (*moottoriliikennetie*) in Finland. You can recognize these from the green signs. On these roads, you can hitch from on-ramps (which often have bus stops that drivers will stop at) and [petrol stations](petrol_station_hitchhiking "petrol stations"){.wikilink}. The cars cannot stop in crossing areas, and some drivers who disobeyed this rule and picked up hitchhikers have been fined.

## Safety

Hitchhiking is quite safe in Finland. As always, common sense is your friend.

## Culture

Hitchhiking used to be popular in the 70\'s and 80\'s, and many drivers will tell the hitchhiker(s) they\'ve hitched themselves back in their days. Nowadays this practice is rather rare among Finns, but there are always some, especially in summer time around music festivals. Hitching in Finland might be difficult during the [winter](winter "winter"){.wikilink} time because lack of daylight. The weather differences in Finland seem to effect greatly how well people pick you up. Sunny weather might help you a great deal because it makes people more happy and open-minded.

Hitchhiking seems to get easier the more North you go in Finland, because towns get smaller and people feel more open to help others. However, the highways are better in the South and there is more traffic.

## Language

The language most people in Finland speak is Finnish which isn\'t a Scandinavian language. The country is officially bilingual as there is a large Swedish-speaking minority on the west coast of Finland. Most people speak English too, so most of the foreign hitchhikers won\'t have communication problems in Finland.

Some older people, however, might have no English language knowledge at all, so you might as well learn some basic Finnish phrases. Note, that with a knowledge of Swedish, however, you can come pretty far (it is useful all over Scandinavia).

#### Useful Finnish expressions for hitchhikers {#useful_finnish_expressions_for_hitchhikers}

- Hello = **Terve**
- Hi = **Moi** *or* **Hei**
- Hitchhiking = **Liftaaminen**
- To hitchhike = **Liftata**
- A ride = **Kyyti**
- Thank you very much = **Kiitos paljon**
- Where are you driving to? = **Minne ajat?**
- I don\'t speak Finnish = **En puhu suomea**

I hitchhiked around the country in the summer of 2024 (Helsinki-Tampere-Turku-Vaasa-Oulu-Pello-Kilpisjarvi. Good 2000km in 5-6 days with stopping in the mentioned places. I would give Finland 2 out of 5. People do not stop almost at all if there is no designated space for it (for example, bus stops). They are afraid of breaking rules and getting punished. Especially, getting out of Tampere (to the south) was mission impossible. After two hours of waiting, I took a local bus to a village, from where I followed the local road. Finland has a few highways, so expect to spend a lot of time in combination with waiting for a ride and driving from one place to another. And finally - the people! My experience is pretty unfriendly and quiet. But those who help and give you a ride are different dimension, excellent!
