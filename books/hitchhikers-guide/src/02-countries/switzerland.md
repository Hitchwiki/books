# Switzerland

**Switzerland** is a landlocked country in [Western Europe](Western_Europe "Western Europe"){.wikilink} it is officially part of the [Schengen Area](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Area"){.wikilink}.

In **Switzerland** people speak four different indigenous languages, depending on the area you are hanging around: [German](Germany "German"){.wikilink} in the north and east, [Italian](Italy "Italian"){.wikilink} in the south, [French](France "French"){.wikilink} in the western part of the country, and Rhaeto-Rumantsch, which is derived from Vulgar Latin, brought by roman soldiers two millenia ago in the mountains of eastern Switzerland.

## Hitchhiking

The [Verein Autostop Schweiz](https://www.autostoppp.ch/en/) organizes the [Swiss Autostop Championship](https://www.autostoppp.ch/en/) yearly since 2014 from [Fribourg](Fribourg "Fribourg"){.wikilink}.

There is a large percentage of foreigners, as well as many tourists. Most people will be able to understand some English (most young people speak it well).

Hitchhiking works very well in **Switzerland**, especially in the mountain regions and small country roads, where it is quite common to pick up hikers and hitchhikers. It is also quite easy to get lifts with truck drivers from [Germany](Germany "Germany"){.wikilink} to [Italy](Italy "Italy"){.wikilink} and back. Thre are not that many service stations along the highways, and some are not open 24 hours. In Switzerland you can find [official hitchhiking spots](Official_Hitchhiking "official hitchhiking spots"){.wikilink} mostly in the form of [benches (\"Mitfahrbänkli\")](Hitchhiking_Bench "benches ("Mitfahrbänkli")"){.wikilink} in rural areas.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Swiss law contains no rule against hitchhiking, only against being on foot where the motor traffic is. [Article 43(3) of the *Strassenverkehrsgesetz* (SVG, SR 741.01)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1959/679_705_685/de) provides that on roads reserved for motor vehicles --- *Autobahnen* and *Autostrassen* --- only the vehicle types the Federal Council names may drive, and that \"entry is prohibited, access being permitted only at the places provided for it\". Going in on foot is therefore out: no thumbing on the carriageway, the hard shoulder or the on-ramp past the green motorway sign, and no walking into a service area from the road behind it. Being dropped at a *Raststätte* and asking drivers there is the normal and lawful way to move along the motorway network. On every other road hitchhiking is legal and unregulated.

## Hitchhiking at the border {#hitchhiking_at_the_border}

Since in not legal to hitch on motorways (petrol stations are allowed though), you should avoid to get dropped at the border. If you\'re hitchhiking with a truck driver into [Italy](Italy "Italy"){.wikilink}, he\'ll maybe need to stop at the border before driving on - but ask him not to say that you\'re a hitchhiker since the border police might tell you to leave the truck and drive you to the bus station instead.

## Camping

In Switzerland, everybody is legally allowed to use pasture and woods to a certain extent, including camping. The same goes for agriculturally not usable space such as public bodies of water, rock, snow or ice.

Sometimes its good to let locals know that youre camping next to their property. If someone reacts bad, just ask another person or people who are on a walk.

## Highway petrol stations {#highway_petrol_stations}

If you type the name of the petrol station in Google Maps you will find where exactly it is. This makes it much more comfortable to hitchhike.
