# French Guiana

**French Guiana** is an overseas region of [France](France "France"){.wikilink} in [South America](South_America "South America"){.wikilink}.

There are borders with [Suriname](Suriname "Suriname"){.wikilink} and [Brazil](Brazil "Brazil"){.wikilink}.

It is generally a safe country (technically an overseas department of [France](France "France"){.wikilink}).\'\'

## Hitchhiking

Even the Lonely Planet says that hitchhiking is doable, so unlike the former British colony [Guyana](Guyana "Guyana"){.wikilink} (which is considered rather unsafe), you can stick out your thumb without too much worries. Naturally, it helps if you can speak French.

\'\'[Wisdomseeker](User:Wisdomseeker "Wisdomseeker"){.wikilink} has hitchhiked in French Guiana - it is certainly \'doable\'.

*[Nveselinov](User:nveselinov "Nveselinov"){.wikilink} has hitchhiked in French Guiana - it is very good. The most of the peoples are very good. I traveled from Kouru to St. George (the border town with Brasil). There were not many cars in the area near the border, but every 3-4th car takes you. I went form Kouru to St. George for 4,5 hours.*

\'\'[Carry_On_My_Wayward_Thumb](User:Carry_On_My_Wayward_Thumb "Carry_On_My_Wayward_Thumb"){.wikilink} has hitchhiked in French Guiana - One of the best places to hitch in all of the mainland Americas. On par with Ecuador and Nicaragua as far as ease and friendliness of your rides. Some cars stopped for me before I even put my thumb out! Only issue is the remoteness east of Cayenne. If you get stranded you will be very far from everything. Definitely bring plenty of water, some snacks and sun protection because even though people are willing to pick you up, there aren\'t many cars going east to the border with Brazil. From St Laurent to Kourou took me three hours, Kourou to St George took me four.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

French Guiana is an overseas department, so the French *code de la route* applies here in full and, as in metropolitan [France](France "France"){.wikilink}, it contains no offence of hitchhiking. What usually restricts a hitchhiker there does not bite here: the department has no *autoroute*, so [article R421-2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006842313), which bars pedestrians from motorways, and article R421-1, which extends that ban to the slip roads, have nothing to apply to. The RN1 towards [Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni](Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni "Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni"){.wikilink} and the RN2 towards [Saint-Georges](Saint-Georges "Saint-Georges"){.wikilink} are ordinary roads, so you may stand on the verge of either. What does apply everywhere is [article R412-36](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006074228/LEGISCTA000006177125/): outside built-up areas a pedestrian keeps to the left-hand edge of the road and walks facing the oncoming traffic. On the dual-carriageway stretches around [Cayenne](Cayenne "Cayenne"){.wikilink}, check the entry signs --- a road signposted as reserved for motor vehicles excludes pedestrians by that sign alone, autoroute or not.
