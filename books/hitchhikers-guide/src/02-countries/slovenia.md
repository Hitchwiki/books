# Slovenia

**Slovenia** is a country in **Central Europe** according to wikipedia, alternatively part of [Southern Europe](Southern_Europe "Southern Europe"){.wikilink}. It is a member state of the [European Union](European_Union "European Union"){.wikilink} as well as the [Schengen Agreement](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"){.wikilink}. Hitchhiking used to be more common here in the past, but it is still very doable and safe. Drivers are happy to see a hitchhiker and will tell you stories about hitchhiking when they were young. People in Slovenia usually speak English (at least the basics) but German and Italian (on the cost) can also come in handy.

Slovenia can offer you a lot to see on its small territory. Geographic landscapes change fast and you can get from the foot of the Alps to the Adriatic sea or the Pannonian basin in about 3h ride.

## Hitchhiking

Hitchiking close to highways will take you far and fast, but if you have enough time, use it to hitchhike on the small roads.

From the capital city [Ljubljana](Ljubljana "Ljubljana"){.wikilink}, it is easy to hitchhike to the **north-east** ([Maribor](Maribor "Maribor"){.wikilink}, [Celje](Celje "Celje"){.wikilink}, [Murska Sobota](Murska_Sobota "Murska Sobota"){.wikilink}, [Vienna](Vienna "Vienna"){.wikilink}, [Graz](Graz "Graz"){.wikilink} ([Austria](Austria "Austria"){.wikilink}), [Hungary](Hungary "Hungary"){.wikilink}), **north-west** ([Jesenice](Jesenice "Jesenice"){.wikilink}, [Bled](Bled "Bled"){.wikilink}, [Villach](Villach "Villach"){.wikilink}, [Salzburg](Salzburg "Salzburg"){.wikilink} ([Austria](Austria "Austria"){.wikilink}), [Germany](Germany "Germany"){.wikilink}), **south-east** ([Zagreb](Zagreb "Zagreb"){.wikilink} ([Croatia](Croatia "Croatia"){.wikilink})) and **south-west** ([Postojna](Postojna "Postojna"){.wikilink}, [Koper](Koper "Koper"){.wikilink}, [Italy](Italy "Italy"){.wikilink}). Your best choice would be to use highway entrances, and to hold a sign marking your destination. For more info, check out the page Hitchwiki Ljubljana. Hitchhiking in the rural areas is even easier, and it is possible to get a pick even on the smallest roads. Highways are always in a good condition, there are also service areas on the motorway.

The highway rest stops, standing with a sign seems to work better than asking people, especially if you don\'t speak Slovenian.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Slovenian law contains no offence of hitchhiking (*avtoštop*), and on ordinary roads it is legal. Article 30 of the *Zakon o pravilih cestnega prometa* (ZPrCP), [published in Uradni list RS 109/2010](https://www.uradni-list.si/_pdf/2010/Ur/u2010109.pdf), is the motorway article: paragraph 1 admits only drivers of motor vehicles that can exceed 60 km/h onto an *avtocesta* or *hitra cesta*, paragraph 2 allows joining and leaving only at the marked junctions, and paragraph 15 states flatly that \"pedestrians must not step onto a motorway or expressway or walk along it\". The exceptions in paragraph 16 are police, accident investigators, medics and firefighters, road inspectors and the like --- not travellers. So the carriageway, the hard shoulder and the slip road are all closed, and what is left on the motorway is the *počivališče* rest areas and their petrol stations, where drivers have stopped anyway. On *glavne* and *regionalne ceste* hitchhiking is unregulated: stand on the verge where a driver can see you and pull over safely.

## Border Crossings {#border_crossings}

Since Slovenia is a part of the Schengen area, borders are not a problem. Usually you just pass them without stopping, but sometimes the police would stop the vehicles and ask for documents (ID is enough for EU citizens). Border crossing often get jammed, especially on the borders with Croatia in summer and the Austrian border, before the Karavanke (Karawanken) tunnel.

When leaving the country, don\'t forget that Hungary doesn\'t use € as their currencies, but **Hungarian Forint**. You can exchange Euros in Slovenian banks or post offices. Croatia uses euro since jan 2023.

If you want to pass the border, but get a pick to towns or villages close by, you can take trains or busses to pass it. It is not very expensive, especially at the weekend.

<https://www.slo-zeleznice.si/en/passenger-transport/travelling-abroad>

The link above takes you to the **Slovenian Railways page**. There you can find all about the train connections with neighbour countries (usually departing from Ljubljana and skipping small stations), as well as local lines, many of which can take you over the border as well. It is also the last way out, if you get stuck somewhere. (stay strong\<3)

For intercity busses check out Arriva, Nomago or Flixbus websites and <https://www.ap-ljubljana.si/> if your starting point is Ljubljana. Different bus companies cover different lines.

**Border towns/ villages that might help:** AUSTRIA (Jesenice, Kranjska gora, Dravograd, Slovenj Gradec, Maribor), CROATIA (Brežice, Krško, Dobova, Piran, Črnomelj, Ptuj), HUNGARY (Murska Sobota, Lendava, Hodoš) and ITALY (Nova Gorica, Sežana, Koper, Bovec, Vipava)

Crossing from Slovenia to Austria : Border crossing is a very luxurious hitching spot, as the traffic slows down to about 10km/h (because of the pay toll for Karavanke tunnel, when the road is busy) so the whole highway sees you clearly. There is also plenty of space for drivers to pick you up.

## Useful information {#useful_information}

As everywhere in Europe, it it not allowed and very dangerous to **cross the highway** in Slovenia. Also be careful about crossing the red lights in Slovenian cities, you\'ll get a 20-40€ fine, if you get caught.

**Wildcamping** in Slovenia is officially not allowed, but the law is usually not enforced. As long as you don\'t make fires and follow basic camping rules, people won\'t mind you. You can also go from house to house and ask for a place to set up the tent in the garden. Couchsurfing, especially in Ljubljana, works fine as well.

**Tips** are not mandatory or expected in restaurants, but it is always nice to tip, if you\'re happy with the service. Slovenians often tip in bars.

There are 2 main words for \"hi\" in Slovenian. **Živjo** (Ž is pronouced /ˈʒeː/) is used in Ljubljana and surrondings, and **Zdravo** is dominant around Maribor. You can make a good impression by knowing the difference between the two;)
