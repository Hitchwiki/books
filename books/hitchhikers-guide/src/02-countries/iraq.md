# Iraq

**Iraq** is a country in the [Middle East](Middle_East "Middle East"){.wikilink}.

Except for the autonomous Kurdistan region, Iraq is a instable place and saftey situation can change very fast.

Note that the Kurdistan Region (KRG) currently includes the provinces of Duhok, Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, and Halabja.

## Hitchhiking

It is important to make inform yourself very good about the current safety situation and about the risks. However Iraq is not as dangerous as it was in the past and it is absolutely possible to Hitchhike there.

**There are Risks connected with Hitchhiking in Iraq and there is no guarantee for the thruth of the Informations on this site, evaluate the risks on your own before you make a decision!**

There are Whatsapp groups with locals and travelers that respond to questions and try to help each other. Most Couchsurfing Hosts or other travelers you meet in Iraq can add you to this groups.

Bring cash in USD or Euro with you as the Exchange Offices do have a better exchange rate than the oficial exchange rate. If you need to withdrawl money, Trade Bank of Iraq ATMs have no fee for foreign cards. But still the exchange rate is worse than the one of the exchange offices. In the country you can almoast never pay with card, carry enough cash.

## Iraqi Kurdistan {#iraqi_kurdistan}

This region is autonomous from the rest of Iraq and is completely safe for hitchhikers. People are warm, hospitable and very western friendly.

However, avoid going to the disputed city of Kirkuk. In the whole country there are plenty of checkpoints which are one of the reasons why the country is so safe. It is easy to avoid going into the cities if you make it clear to the driver. Its also a good idea to avoid remote mountainous areas near the Turkish and Iranian border because of the ongoing conflicts with Turkey and Iran. They used to drop Bombs in the Border regions.

### Safety

As said above, the KRG (Kurdistan Regional Government) is largely safe, and will probably continue to be so. The situation doesn\'t appear to be likely to change any time soon, however a people surrounded by enemies get their fair share of trouble from time-to-time. Monitor news reports while you are in Kurdistan.

### Entry

You can get a 30 Day E-Visa for around 75 USD on the oficial KRG website. A Visa on Arrival is no longer available even though many people don\'t know this yet. However you get your E-Visa Instantly after applying so you can do it at the Border, but it is necessary to have a credit card to pay the fee in IQD online. It is possible to extend the Visa maximal 2 times in government offices in different citys for another fee of 75 USD. This Visa is oficialy only valid for Kurdistan but in most cases they let travellers from Europe and America pass to Mosul and from there they always accept a Kurdish Visa at the checkpoints. In rare cases they send you back but then you can always try at a different checkpoint and they will let you in. You can even fly from [Baghdad](Baghdad "Baghdad"){.wikilink} with a Kurdish visa whitout any problems.

**From Turkey** There is an agreement between the two countries that individuals cannot cross the border on foot. Therefore, there are a lot of taxis. However, if you wait for a while at the border and explain that you want to *otostop* the Turkish guards may pay for your taxi (if they like you). This happened to hitchwiki member Vlad. There are also a lot of people buying a lot of cigarettes in the Duty free shop but there is a limit how much one person can buy, therefore there are many people interested in having a second person that can buy cigarettes for them.

*If you decide to take a taxi, you might as well take it from [Cizre](Cizre "Cizre"){.wikilink} (50 km from the border) instead of [Silopi](Silopi "Silopi"){.wikilink} (10 km from the border) as the taxi prices are the same (20 TL). When I arrived in Iraq my Taxi driver dropped me off just after the border, he told me I would have to take another taxi to get to Zakho but the price would only be 1 TL - that was not true, the taxi was 4000 Iraqi Dinaar (about 6,5 TL). Anyway, don\'t take another taxi, it\'s just 10 kilometers to the town, you can walk or hitch it.* \--[Jakobwithk](User:Jakobwithk "Jakobwithk"){.wikilink} 19:29, 12 January 2012 (CET)

**From Iran** At the Pervez Khan boarder to the west of [Kermanshah](Kermanshah "Kermanshah"){.wikilink} you can freely enter by foot. Border guards will look surprised to meet somebody who is not smuggling goods from Iran into Iraq, especially if you\'re not from the Middle East. Luggage will be fully checked twice, but when they notice you are there to visit their beautiful country they will give you a big smile and let you through in no time. Last verified on 22nd of October 2016.

### Exit

**Iraqi Kurdistan- Turkey**

On the way back, the Iraq side is walkable by foot, but when you reach the Turkish side they expect that you have a taxi. When they see you\'re clearly a backpacker type, they might be more lenient and arrange for a passing truck to take you through. In short, easier to cross the Turkish side without a taxi on the way back from Iraq. You may also find mini vans (dolmuses) willing to take you for free on the Iraqi side, you can normally find them in petrol stations filling up their tanks before they head over to the more expensive Turkey. However, be warned that they offer you a free ride because they want to use you to smuggle cigarettes, tea and other goods across to Turkey. The consequences of getting caught is not known but checks are usually fairly strict because of the P.K.K conflict in the Sirnak district and checks could take upto 5 hours or more. These mini-vans are usually happy to take you upto Silopi or Cizre at least for free.

**Iraqi Kurdistan - Iran**

There are two main border crossings between Iraqi Kurdistan and Iran: Piranshahr (the more popular of both) and Penjwin-Marivan. A third point exists between Tawela and Nowsud, but some locals say it is only for trucks and not specially safe - although this has not been not confirmed.

[Penjwin-Marivan](Penjwin-Marivan "Penjwin-Marivan"){.wikilink} - the immigration office is somewhat randomly located on the right side (as you arrive) - walk towards the big gate with Iraqi/Kurdish flags and then take a right along the Duty Free shop. Once you get inside (it looks like a waiting room at the dentist\'s), locate the \"exit\" window, put your passport on top of the stack of passports there, sit down and wait for the officer to mispronounce your name.

April 2014: *[Rovingsnails](User:Rovingsnails "Rovingsnails"){.wikilink} crossed this border without any problems. From Said Sadq a policeman stopped a bus for them, directly to Marivan (Iran).*

The Iranian side was fairly quick and without searches. He was asked a bunch of \"friendly\" questions by the very-not-obvious secret police, incl. \"What\'s your religion?\" \...*

### Hitching

Iraqi Kurdistan is probably one of the easiest places in the world to hitchhike. Almost every car will stop for you, often it is not even necessary to signal for a ride. People are very interested in meeting foreigners, so it might help to show that you are a tourist. Many people will want to take you to bus stations or taxi stands. Many people don\'t understand the principles of hitchhiking and don\'t understand that it helps you if they take you only half the distance. They will all offer you to take you to your destination for money and when you say no they will want to go. You will have to make clear that they should take you only where they go anyway and everyone will be very happy to help you. Also people don\'t understand that someone has money but can\'t afford a Taxi. When you say you don\'t have money they will offer you to give you money for food etc.

Kurdish people are very friendly and will go out of their way to help you. Its very normal to be picked up and invited to stay at the family home of the driver or at least get invited for food. Expect people that don\'t speak English to call their friends to translate. Many people there have relatives in Europe. If you use google translate choose Kurdish (sorani).

### Women

It is fully possible for a solo woman to hitchhike alone in Kurdistan, but staying safe might require a little more care than in other countries. Claiming to be married doesnt seem to help much here. Taxi drivers may stop and tell you they will take you for free, and seem completely well-intentioned, indicating they are going that way anyway, until you get into the car at which point they will let you know they are looking for another form of payment. As to other rides, try to signal cars carrying women, children, or two or more business-type men - they keep each other honest. This post is not meant to discourage women from hitching here, just to pay more attention than in other places - dont be afraid to turn down rides if you have any sense of a bad feeling. In my experience ( [alyssa](User:notapalooka "alyssa"){.wikilink} ), every time I got into a car with a single man in Kurdistan he ended up hitting on me, usually in a very direct way. However despite this I did not feel unsafe, and when I asked the drivers to stop and let me out they did so. It must be remembered that in this culture it is quite bizaare for a respectable women to be traveling alone, let alone standing on this side of the road asking for a free ride. You just have to make your intentions known early on.

### Checkpoints

One of the reasons Kurdistan is safe at the moment is the huge amounts of Kurdish military checkpoints around the country. They\'re mostly looking out for Arab and Shia Iraqis entering the area, but they\'ll take an interest in foreign hitchers for sure. They\'ll check your passport and sometimes take you into a the chiefs office for questioning. It\'s not an interrogation, they just want to know why your here and where you want to go. Assure them that you are a tourist that wants to see the country and that you\'re not going south of Kurdistan, or to Mosul or Kirkuk. They\'ll usually let you go with no fuss. Sometimes they\'ll even make you a chai and give you food or give you some cold water.

Checkpoint stops are pretty regular and there\'s no communication between each one, so be prepared to be stopped a lot in Kurdistan. It\'s usually not for longer than five to ten minutes at a time and often they don\'t stop you at all. The guards are likely to smile at you and say, \"welcome to our country\"

You should also note that the main road between Erbil and Zakho actually passes very close to the non-Kurdish city of Mosul.

Blue license plates belong to Kurdish and government officials respectively.

### Camping

[Camping](Camping "Camping"){.wikilink} is possible in Kurdistan and safe but you should make sure nobody sees you. People in Kurdistan don\'t understand that someone wants to camp voluntary. [User:Hitchboy](User:Hitchboy "User:Hitchboy"){.wikilink} asked a police officer wheter it is legal to camp and they took him to the Police headquarter and Interogated him for hours. They asked what he was doing here etc. However the officers are always friendly and offer food and tea and even appologize for what they have to do.

## Federal Iraq {#federal_iraq}

Under the name Federal Iraq is meant all the rest of Iraq.

### Safety {#safety_1}

At the moment Federal Iraq is quite safe. There where no Terrorist attacks on civilians and Kidnapping cases for years. However there is a very small risk to get into a clash between individuals or between individuals and security forces. But this happens very rarely and the chance to be at exactly at the wrong place in exactly the wrong moment is very small. In the desert and especially along the syrian border cells of daesh (ISIS) and other militias are still operating but the cities are largely safe.

### Entry {#entry_1}

If you enter through a federal Iraq Border like [Baghdad](Baghdad "Baghdad"){.wikilink} Airport you will have to apply for a Iraq E-Visa in advance. This Visa is valid for Iraq and Kurdistan, however you have to enter into federal Iraq first and then you can go to Kurdistan from there. If you enter Kurdistan first with an federal Iraq E-Visa you will still only get the Kurdish Visa in your Passport and therefore it is a waste of money. This happens for example when you try to enter Kurdistan from Turkey or through Erbil Airport. In this case you need a Kurdish Visa only. If you enter from Turkey or from Kurdistan you will most probably have a Kurdish Visa. This Visa is oficialy only valid for Kurdistan but in most cases they let travelers pass to Mosul and from there they always accept a Kurdish Visa at the checkpoints. In rare cases they send you back but then you can always try at a different checkpoint and they will let you in. You can even fly from [Baghdad](Baghdad "Baghdad"){.wikilink} with a Kurdish visa whitout any problems.

### Hitching {#hitching_1}

Iraq is very easy to Hitchhike. People are very interested in meeting foreigners, so it might help to show that you are a tourist. Many people don\'t understand the principles of hitchhiking and don\'t understand that it helps you if they take you only part of the distance. They will all offer you to take you to your destination for money and when you say no they will want to leave you. You will have to make clear that they should take you only where they go anyway and everyone will be very happy to help you. Also people don\'t understand that someone has money but can\'t afford a Taxi. When you say you don\'t have money they will often offer you to give you money for food etc.

Iraqis are very friendly and will go out of their way to help you. Its very normal to be picked up and invited to stay at the family home of the driver or at least get invited for food and drinks. Expect people that don\'t speak English to call their friends to translate. Most people don\'t speak english so google Translate is very helpfull.
