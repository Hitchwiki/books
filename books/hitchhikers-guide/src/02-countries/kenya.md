# Kenya

**Kenya** is a country in East [Africa](Africa "Africa"){.wikilink}. It has a large variety of tribes living in different areas of the country, with the Kikuyu tribe being the biggest. The famous Maasai tribe lives near the border with [Tanzania](Tanzania "Tanzania"){.wikilink}.

## Trans-African Highway {#trans_african_highway}

This road, going from [Cairo](Cairo "Cairo"){.wikilink}, [Egypt](Egypt "Egypt"){.wikilink}, to [Cape Town](Cape_Town "Cape Town"){.wikilink}, [South Africa](South_Africa "South Africa"){.wikilink}, leads through Kenya as well. In the north of the country, it is little more than a dust track through the desert, and this used to be a dangerous part of the road to travel on, with several hold-ups reported. [Gerben](User:gerbennap "Gerben"){.wikilink} travelled here in the summer of 2010, and he was told the banditry had drastically decreased after negotiations on illegal weapons possession in the region had been conducted by local authorities.

Take care with the rainy season in this part of the country as well, as the dusty roads quickly turn into slippery mud roads once the rain starts pouring down. An asphalt (or *tarmac*, as it is called here) road to the border at [Moyale](Moyale "Moyale"){.wikilink} is under construction, but will take at least until 2015 to complete.

Update 2018: The tarmac road is complete and in perfect condition. However, there are tons of speed bumps which makes hitchhiking quite easy despite the high speeds cars can travel on it.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Kenya against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on. If you can read the official text, please replace this paragraph with the provision it turns on and a link to it.
