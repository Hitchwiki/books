# Jersey

**Jersey** is southernmost and biggest island of the [Channel Islands](Channel_Islands "Channel Islands"){.wikilink} group just offshore Brittany and Normandy in [France](France "France"){.wikilink}. Despite its location near the french coast, the Bailiwick of Jersey remains a private property to the British Crown; therefore it holds an independent status inside the [United Kingdom](United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"){.wikilink}. The capital and main harbour is St. Helier.

The longest straight journey you could undertake are some 11 miles (about 17km).

## Hitchhiking

The island is big enough for cars to circulate, and a maximal speed of 40mph could facilitate hitchhiking.

Hitchhiking on the island is very easy. Traffic isn\'t going very fast so it\'s easy to find a place where people can stop safely. With a very low crime-rate on the island, people are not scared to pick up strangers.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Hitchhiking is not mentioned in Jersey law, and there is no motorway on the island for anyone to be banned from: the [Road Traffic (Jersey) Law 1956](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/Pages/25.550.aspx) has no motorway or expressway class at all, and nothing in it regulates where a pedestrian may stand or forbids asking a driver for a lift. The one article that sounds as though it is about hitchhiking is not --- article 54(1) fines you for taking hold of or getting onto a vehicle *while in motion on any road* for the purpose of being drawn or carried, which is about hanging on to a moving one, so wait until the driver has actually stopped. What does constrain the practice is the driver\'s side of it: article 55 makes it unlawful to let a vehicle stand on a road in a position causing unnecessary obstruction or likely to cause danger, so pick a spot with room to pull in properly. The waiting and parking restrictions the parishes make by Order under articles 59 and 62 bite the same way, and are signed where they apply.

## Hitchhiking in {#hitchhiking_in}

The Condor ferries to the island are pay per passenger, so it\'s not hitchable. It may be possible to hitch private ships or fishing boats.

[Ferries](Ferries "Ferries"){.wikilink} to the island leave from: [Portsmouth](Portsmouth "Portsmouth"){.wikilink}, [Poole](Poole "Poole"){.wikilink}, [St Malo](St_Malo "St Malo"){.wikilink}, [Cherbourg](Cherbourg "Cherbourg"){.wikilink}, [Granville](Granville "Granville"){.wikilink} and [Carteret](Carteret "Carteret"){.wikilink}.

If you plan to hitch a ride on the boat to your destination make sure not to check-in any luggage, as you will have to exit with all the foot passengers to collect it.

## Personal expierence {#personal_expierence}

In May 2024 I spent 3 days hiking, hitchhiking and riding busses on the island. On the smaller roads I would walk untill I\'d hear a car coming my way and give it the thumb. On the bigger A-roads I would find any place where I was reasonable visable and hitch from there. The walking/hitching didn\'t always work, but that\'s mainly due to low volumes of trafffic. Standing on the A-roads I never waited more than 10 minutes. Many drivers would take a detour to bring me exactly where I wanted to go.
