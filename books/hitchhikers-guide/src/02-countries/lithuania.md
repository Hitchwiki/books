# Lithuania

**Lithuania** is a member state of the [European Union](European_Union "European Union"){.wikilink} as well as the [Schengen Agreement](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"){.wikilink}. It has borders to [Latvia](Latvia "Latvia"){.wikilink}, [Belarus](Belarus "Belarus"){.wikilink}, [Poland](Poland "Poland"){.wikilink} and [Kaliningrad](Kaliningrad "Kaliningrad"){.wikilink} ([Russia](Russia "Russia"){.wikilink}).

## Hitchhiking

Like the other Baltics it is a nice country to hitchhike. There are many active hitchhikers. Hitchhiking is fairly common in Lithuania you can regularly spot people hitchhiking to travel, some also hitchhike as a freetime activity. There are also people active in clubs like [Vilnius Hitchhiking Club](Vilnius_Hitchhiking_Club "Vilnius Hitchhiking Club"){.wikilink} and organizing races.

![Agne hitchhiking in Lithuania.](Agne-from-vilnius-to-radviliskis.jpg "Agne hitchhiking in Lithuania."){width="200"}

But when going from [Latvia](Latvia "Latvia"){.wikilink} to [Poland](Poland "Poland"){.wikilink} try to get a direct hitch to the Polish border − it\'s rather hard getting a car on this motorway − it\'s built for quickly crossing Lithuania!

Often there are bus stops where many hitchhikers try to get rides. Don\'t be scared to walk next to a motorway, especially if it\'s next to a city. The locals and even schoolkids do it, so drivers are used to it, and you are more likely to stop someone.

The only problem when hitchhiking in Lithuania is Kaunas, because of its infrastructure, if you\'re dropped of there on a motorway, you\'re likely to walk for a couple of miles.

Get yourself a SIM card for your mobile phone if you want to stay in touch with Lithuanians. The cheapest start at the equivalent of EUR 0,30 at most kiosks.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Lithuanian law contains no offence of hitchhiking (*autostopas*), and on ordinary roads it is legal. [Item 174 of the *Kelių eismo taisyklės*](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/2a948a80506e11e485f39f55fd139d01/) lists what is forbidden on a road signed *Automagistralė* or *Automobilių kelias*, and 174.1 begins with pedestrians --- no walking, no animals, no bicycles or mopeds, nothing but cars, motorcycles and the like capable of at least 60 km/h. Item 174.2 forbids stopping or standing except in the specially designated areas, 174.3 bans U-turns and reversing, and 174.4 allows joining and leaving only at the places provided, so the slip road is closed to you as well as the carriageway. The designated *aikštelės* and their petrol stations are therefore the only spot on those roads where a lift can lawfully begin. Everywhere else --- the *krašto* and *rajoniniai keliai* that carry most of the traffic --- thumbing from the verge breaks no rule.

Like many countries in Eastern Europe hitchhiking on the motorways isn\'t really forbidden (it\'s forbidden to walk on motorway), so the advice would be **not** to hitchhike from petrol stations.

## Personal Experiences {#personal_experiences}

<div class="hw-exp-text">

Hitchhiking was nice. Waiting time is not long (30min-90min). It\'s easy to find someone who are going to another country (Latvia or Poland) so you can pass by Lithuania easily. The people don\'t always speak English but they are quite friendly. A lot of worker vans are ready to get you to another city. The highways are good, but sometimes there are no safety pocket or place to stand, so you need to walk untill another smote (up to 5-10km).

`<span class="hw-exp-author">`{=html}[Finn](User:FSM "Finn"){.wikilink}`</span>`{=html} `<span class="hw-exp-date">`{=html}2024-2026`</span>`{=html}

<div class="hw-exp-text">

Lithuania is a great country for hitchhiking. Not many people are doing it nowadays, but Lithuanians still understand hitchhiking very well and are open towards it. Especially since the release of the popular \"Pietinia kronikas\" movie in 2024, which depicts life in Lithuania during the chaotic 90s and includes scenes of hitchhiking. Pretty much everyone in Lithuania is familiar with that one!

Lithuania is the kind of country where you will get picked up by the whole spectrum of society - young, old, poor, rich, Lithuanians, Ukrainian refugees, central asian immigrants, tourists and everything in between. Younger people speak English well, older people only Lithuanian and Russian (pro tip: do NOT compare Lithuania with Russia in any way, especially the language). The language is very strange, but even just learning a few basic words and phrases of Lithuanian will get you very positive reactions. You will find the most interesting rides along the E67, where it is not too difficult to get a lift all the way to Latvia, Estonia or Poland. On the countryside, hitchhiking from one village to the next is trivial, often the first car that finds you will pick you up.
