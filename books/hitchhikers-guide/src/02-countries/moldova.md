# Moldova

Hitchhiking is very easy in **Moldova**. You\'ll see many locals hitching rides between towns, who usually pay for it. Nevertheless, most travelers were never asked to pay.

## Hitchhiking

Even within the capital [Chişinău](Chişinău "Chişinău"){.wikilink} it\'s possible to hitch rides.

*Hitchhiking* while standing on the side of the road and raising your hand (so that the hand is parallel with the ground) is used as the sign for locals hitching rides, mostly just some kilometres to the next village. Most drivers understand raising the thumb as *autostop* and barely ever ask for money, if you\'re a foreign traveler it could be more common that drivers will buy you food or drinks.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Moldova against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on. If you can read the official text, please replace this paragraph with the provision it turns on and a link to it.

To hitchhike in Moldova is not prohibited or outlawed, so you can freely hitchhike everywhere without any restrictions.

## Language controversy {#language_controversy}

Though the official language of [Moldova](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_language) is called *Moldovan*, it is really Romanian, identical to the Romanian spoken in [Romania](Romania "Romania"){.wikilink}. Romanian language origins can be traced from the Latin language, so if you get to know any romanic language like French, Italian or Spanish you can communicate with people at a basic level. Also the majority of people understand and speak Russian as well - for some it\'s their only language.

## Border Crossing {#border_crossing}

They are allowed to stay up to 90 days without visa and registration.

While entering **Moldova**, you could face some problems when you entered from Ukraine via the autonomous [Transnistria](Transnistria "Transnistria"){.wikilink} region. The Transnistrian border is not controlled by Moldovan authorities, which means you won\'t automatically get an entry stamp when entering Moldova here. This can cause a lot of problems when leaving Moldova via other border checkpoint than Transnistrian. For more information see the Transnistria page. At least on some border crossings (like [Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan border crossing](Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan_border_crossing "Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan border crossing"){.wikilink}) it\'s possible to ask for a Moldovan entry stamp.

### From/Into [Ukraine](Ukraine "Ukraine"){.wikilink} {#frominto_ukraine}

- **South-East**: If you are traveling e.g. from [Odesa](Odessa "Odesa"){.wikilink} into [Moldova](Moldova "Moldova"){.wikilink}, best option is to take the Ukrainian M-15 motorway south-west and cross borders at the [Palanca-Maiaky border crossing](Palanca-Maiaky_border_crossing "Palanca-Maiaky border crossing"){.wikilink}. From the border, you could already try to get rides directly to [Chişinău](Chişinău "Chişinău"){.wikilink} or further. Walking over the border checkpoint is no problem, just keep in mind that you have to walk 3 km from the Ukrainian to the Moldovan side, so best is to hitch a ride just after the first checkpoint towards the next. Another option is the [Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan border crossing](Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan_border_crossing "Pervomaisk-Kuchurhan border crossing"){.wikilink}. This is a route used by many locals. It\'s also possible to cross on foot.
- **North-West**: When traveling from **Moldova** towards [Ivano-Frankivsk](Ivano-Frankivsk "Ivano-Frankivsk"){.wikilink} or [Lviv](Lviv "Lviv"){.wikilink}, you have two border crossings to choose. The best option could be to hitchhike via [Edineț](Edineț "Edineț"){.wikilink}, [Briceni](Briceni "Briceni"){.wikilink} towards [Lipcani](Lipcani "Lipcani"){.wikilink} to take the [Criva-Mamalyha border crossing](Criva-Mamalyha_border_crossing "Criva-Mamalyha border crossing"){.wikilink} and go on via [Chernovtsy](Chernivtsi "Chernovtsy"){.wikilink}. Walking over by foot is no problem there, but you might have to wait for a lift some time.

### From/Into [Romania](Romania "Romania"){.wikilink} {#frominto_romania}

- [Galaţi-Giugiuleşti border crossing‎](Galaţi-Giugiuleşti_border_crossing‎ "Galaţi-Giugiuleşti border crossing‎"){.wikilink} is the southernmost border-crossing, located in the Danube Delta. It is only three kilometers from the nearest crossing into Ukraine.

- [Leușeni-Albița border crossing](Leușeni-Albița_border_crossing "Leușeni-Albița border crossing"){.wikilink} is one of the most used to enter into/exit Moldova. Is located in the center of Moldova, and it is located close to [Chișinău](Chișinău "Chișinău"){.wikilink}. You can\'t walk across this border crossing, but the border guards will usually help you to find a lift.
