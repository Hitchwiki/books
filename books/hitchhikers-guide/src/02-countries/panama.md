# Panama

Panama has borders with [Costa Rica](Costa_Rica "Costa Rica"){.wikilink} to the North and [Colombia](Colombia "Colombia"){.wikilink} to the South. However, there are no roads to [Colombia](Colombia "Colombia"){.wikilink}.

## Hitchhiking

![Hitchhikers [Katja and Augustas](Katja_and_Augustas "Katja and Augustas"){.wikilink} on the road in Panama.](Katja_and_Augustas_hitchiking_in_Panama.jpg "Hitchhikers Katja and Augustas on the road in Panama."){width="230"} \'\'\'*\' is a country in [Central America](Central_America "Central America"){.wikilink} where hitchhiking happens most readily. Roads north of Panama city are brilliant. Roads to the south are atrocious, and worsen as you approach [Yavisa](Yavisa "Yavisa"){.wikilink}. Hitch-hiking is called*viajar a lift*, you can say you are*buscando un lift hacia\...*(*looking for a lift towards\...\'\').

To get to Colombia see the article on the [Darien Gap](Darién_Gap "Darien Gap"){.wikilink}

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Panama against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on. If you can read the official text, please replace this paragraph with the provision it turns on and a link to it.
