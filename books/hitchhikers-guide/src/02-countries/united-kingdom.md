# United Kingdom

The **United Kingdom** is a country in [Nothern Europe](Europe "Nothern Europe"){.wikilink} which forms the so-called [Common Travel area](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Travel_Area) with [Ireland](Ireland "Ireland"){.wikilink} and is not part of the [Schengen Agreement](Schengen_Agreement "Schengen Agreement"){.wikilink}. It is connected to mainland Europe via the [English channel](English_channel "English channel"){.wikilink}. As of 2020, it is no longer part of the [European Union](European_Union "European Union"){.wikilink}.

## Hitchhiking

Hitchhiking in the United Kingdom is feasible although the British are a little surprised (especially in [England](England "England"){.wikilink}) to see people still doing it these days since it has become a dying trend largely due to the safety worries, insurance issues and very busy roads, but also extremely cheap coach fares. You will still manage a ride somehow but you really need to be **at the right spot.** Finding a safe spot where drivers can pull over easily can be crucial here - British people usually don\'t like to stop in bad places, so make it easy for them to pick you up!

There are people in the UK who hitchhiking to travel, some also hitchhike as a freetime activity. [Races](Races "Races"){.wikilink} are regularly organized mostly by university societies.

Hitching in [Scotland](Scotland "Scotland"){.wikilink} or [Wales](Wales "Wales"){.wikilink} tends to be more easy than in southern [England](England "England"){.wikilink} although this depends from county to county. For example, the rural mentality in the [South West](South_West_England "South West"){.wikilink} makes it a lot more easier than trying to hitch in the [South East](South_East_England "South East"){.wikilink}. It seems the closer you are to big cities, especially London, the harder it is. Furthermore, especially in the South of England, people have some kind of you-only-get-what-you-merit-(equals: work for)-attitude, which is why they sometimes react with unfriendliness to the idea of hitching. On the other hand, many students hitched in the 70s and 80s and they often give lifts now that they are older.

When traveling a long distance on the motorways it is best to stick to the service stations as getting a lift on a slip road will take an exceptionally longer time. Don\'t get dropped at any motorway intersection junctions (*M25/M4*, *M4/M5* etc.) as traffic will be going too fast to stop and you will probably be picked up by the police. In case you *do* get dropped there your best option is probably to start walking a bit away from the motorway, and if you\'re a foreigner, attach a sign to your backpack saying e.g. *from Holland*.

As A-roads, however, are a replacement for motorways in more rural areas, people basically go at the same speed, and it is quite difficult and dangerous to flag somebody down.

If you\'re hitchhiking long distances and considering making a sign, in general people use the motorway names rather than the city names. For example, from [London](London "London"){.wikilink} to [Cornwall](South_West_England "Cornwall"){.wikilink} have a sign saying *M4*. But if you are hitching on a motorway with multiple cities (i.e. *M1*) it is an idea to use a sign with the city name (e.g. [Nottingham](Nottingham "Nottingham"){.wikilink}, [Sheffield](Sheffield "Sheffield"){.wikilink}).

As a final note, rides are called lifts and trucks are called lorries:)

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Hitchhiking is legal in the United Kingdom and no law forbids asking a stranger for a lift; motorways are the exception. [Rule 253 of the Highway Code](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/motorways-253-to-273) states that motorways must not be used by pedestrians, citing the Motorways Traffic (England and Wales) Regulations 1982 (regs 3(d), 4 and 11) and, in Scotland, the Motorways Traffic (Scotland) Regulations 1995 --- the ban covers the carriageway, the hard shoulder and the slip roads. Rule 271 adds that nobody may stop on a carriageway, emergency area, hard shoulder, slip road, central reservation or verge except in an emergency, so a lift cannot lawfully be picked up on the motorway itself. Motorway service areas are private land reached off the motorway rather than part of it, so asking drivers there is lawful, though the operator can ask you to leave. On A-roads and everything below them, stand safely off the carriageway and thumb: there is no offence in it.

Apart from motorways, you can also hitch on the so-called A-roads, where - legally - you can stand on the side lane.

Like anywhere else, it is illegal to walk on motorways, and so hitchhiking is best done from the bottom of [slip roads](slip_road "slip road"){.wikilink} and at [service stations](rest_area "service stations"){.wikilink} (at discretion of the owner). Highways Agency officials may question you if you hitch from sliproads on motorways as technically you are not allowed to do this, but the police will not pay any interest; the Highways Agency staff are more pedantic than the police regarding that.

## Service stations hitchiking method {#service_stations_hitchiking_method}

Stick to motorway services, as lot of traffic is passing, filling up their tanks. Talk with drivers who are filling up their tanks in petrol station. It will help if you will have a sign, or if you don\'t have one, have some kind of paper map, because you will look more trustable from distance. Thumbing near the motorway services exit also works well.

If there are a lot of traffic coming through petrol station, or in general, don\'t waste time going to speak to drivers in rest area, they will pass you at some point. Try asking drivers if you are in good place for heading that direction, for example: Strazdas007 was stuck on Maidstone services while trying to get to the North, as all drivers where going South, or just around the corner, so he just went back to Dover port. If you are in bad service station for your direction, don\'t hesitate going back to last good spot, or possibly good spot. Try to look up service stations on maps or gps. A Collins road map of the UK (+ Ireland) can be found in book stores for \~£5 and is very useful here since you don\'t want to miss the last possible service sttation. Go for the popular ones if you have a choice, like: Starbucks over Costa, as it is with petrol stations - Shell over BP.

Also, with some luck, you can even get job offers (Strazdas007 got offer from driver to work as roofpainter or smth like that in service station near Leeds).

## Climate

The UK is infamous for its rainy [weather](weather "weather"){.wikilink}. The oceanic climate makes the weather changeable from one day to the next and causes comparatively long but light phases of rain. This doesn\'t mean it\'s always raining, but it *can* always rain. Be prepared and always have waterproof clothes with you. The rainiest months are October−January. As a rule of thumb, one could say the further west and the higher the elevation, the greater the rainfall. Contradicting the cliché, some parts of south and west England are surprisingly dry and may even have drought problems in summer.

An advantage of the mild oceanic climate is that it does not get extremely cold or hot.

## Hitchhiking a ferry {#hitchhiking_a_ferry}

There is a map of ferry routes on [GreenTraveller.co.uk](https://www.greentraveller.co.uk/map-of-uk-ferry-routes). Roughly, there are ferries

- from Dover to Calais (probably the most common route)
- from East England to Netherlands
- from South England to France
- from Wales/West England/Scotland to Ireland

Some ferries allow extra car passengers to turn up for free at the port (like Dover-\>Calas). Others demand an extra fee (often making paying a foot passenger ticket the cheapest option). If you get onto the ferry as a foot passenger, you can still ask around on the ferry and hitch a lift out in someone\'s car. This is often a better idea than trying to wait outside the port when you arrive.

You can read more about hitchiking ferries below, on the [Dover](Dover "Dover"){.wikilink} page, on the [Holyhead](Holyhead "Holyhead"){.wikilink} page, on [List_of_ferries_that_can_be_hitchhiked#Great_Britain](List_of_ferries_that_can_be_hitchhiked#Great_Britain "List_of_ferries_that_can_be_hitchhiked#Great_Britain"){.wikilink}, on [Portsmouth](Portsmouth "Portsmouth"){.wikilink}.

## Hitchhiking towards [Western Europe](Western_Europe "Western Europe"){.wikilink} {#hitchhiking_towards_western_europe}

![[Carl](User:Carl "Carl"){.wikilink} and his hitch partner Naomi leaving [Cardiff](Cardiff "Cardiff"){.wikilink} for [Morocco](Morocco "Morocco"){.wikilink}.](Carl_Nai_Maroc.jpg "Carl and his hitch partner Naomi leaving Cardiff for Morocco."){width="300"} [Hitchhiking the English Channel](English_channel "Hitchhiking the English Channel"){.wikilink} *from* [England](England "England"){.wikilink} to [France](France "France"){.wikilink} is possible. Just hitchhike in direction of [Dover](Dover "Dover"){.wikilink} along the *M20* in the [South East](South_East_England "South East"){.wikilink} to reach the ferries or [The Channel Tunnel](Channel_Tunnel "The Channel Tunnel"){.wikilink}. Once you\'re there, you could either try to find a lift in Dover or in a more relaxed atmosphere on one of the services along the M20, e.g. Maidstone Services (note however that a lot of people on this service station are locals, so you might be better off with a truck driver, they are parked behind the restaurant).

Truckers who cross the channel through the Eurotunnel (train) cannot take an extra person, but those who go by ferry in Dover can. If you\'re lucky you can even eat for free in the truckers\' restaurant on the ferry! If you can\'t get a ride from a service station before Dover, wait by the BP station just over a mile before the ferry port in Dover. See the [English channel](English_channel "English channel"){.wikilink} page.

You can also hitch from the Immingham docks near Grimsby to [Zeebrugge](Zeebrugge "Zeebrugge"){.wikilink} on a truck ferry. Hitching from Scotland to [Scandinavia](Scandinavia "Scandinavia"){.wikilink} is also possible, see [Scrabster](Scrabster "Scrabster"){.wikilink}. Due to fears of terrorism and illegal immigration authorities may ask you not to wait near the docks.

Another option is the various ferry routes from Portsmouth (to Caen, Bilbao, St Malo, Cherbourg, Le Havre). The only regular route is to Caen, and drivers must pay an extra £40-60 for an extra undeclared passenger (first 2 are free) (accurate 2026) (roughly the same price as a foot passenger ticket), so it sounds like it would be difficult to do for free (unless you are alone and find a car with 1 passenger).

## Hitchhiking from [Western Europe](Western_Europe "Western Europe"){.wikilink} {#hitchhiking_from_western_europe}

Hitchiking [across the English Channel](English_channel "across the English Channel"){.wikilink} *to* Great Britain is possible. When coming from [France](France "France"){.wikilink}, make sure you stay on the A26. Loads of truck drivers are heading from [Switzerland](Switzerland "Switzerland"){.wikilink}, [Luxembourg](Luxembourg "Luxembourg"){.wikilink} and [Germany](Germany "Germany"){.wikilink} to Great Britain. So if someone offers you a ride to [Lille](Lille "Lille"){.wikilink}, make sure you stay on the A26 or you might get stuck.

If you\'re dropped off in Calais, you can start hitchhiking at the bottom of the bridge before the check-in. The price is the same for a car, no matter how many passengers (also in Dunkerque). People who booked in advance indicated how many passengers they would have, so it could be better not to mention at the check-in that you\'re a hitchhiker (?). A one way ticket as a foot passenger is € 40. On the parking lot where everyone\'s waiting to board the ferry, you can often find another lift that goes in your direction. Just show your sign to all the waiting truckers/cars.

If coming from Zeebrugge note that the truckers usually can have two passengers on the manifest so if you travel this way you don\'t need to pay for a ferry ticket. You might be better off hitching with a car, because truck drivers are often more suspicious of people who want to be taken across the channel - their livelihood depends on it, as UK police tend to arrest anybody who knowingly or not help illegal immigrants to enter the UK.

## Hitchhiking from or towards [Ireland](Ireland "Ireland"){.wikilink} {#hitchhiking_from_or_towards_ireland}

There are ferries from [Wales](Wales "Wales"){.wikilink} ([Holyhead](Holyhead "Holyhead"){.wikilink} in the North and [Fishguard](Fishguard "Fishguard"){.wikilink} in the South), from Scotland (Troon, Cairnryan, Stranraer) and from England (around Liverpool). Currently, it might only be possible to get across without paying if you can find a sympathetic driver who allows you to hide in their vehicle, as every passenger pays separately.

From Birkenhead there is a ferry with DFDS seaways that goes either directly to Dublin or Belfast. Two ferries per day, one in the morning and one in the evening. If you can find a driver who has already paid for his car + 1, then it is possible to amend that ticket and check in with the driver for only 10 euros. It would still be paying, but on the ferry you get a dinner buffet and breakfast buffet.

Your driver will also get a cabin with his ticket with four bunkbeds in it, so you will depending on wether your driver wants you in the cabin or not, get a night on real sheets. (Cheapest alternative I have been able to come across if you are still to do it in any legal way. Credit to SamanthaofTarth on the westeros internet forum for this initial info.)

(If you decide to pay, consider getting a Megabus for 25 Pound from London to Rosslare, ferry included, as this is already cheaper then the foot passenger ticket for the ferry only. Alternatively, there sometimes are decent Rail & Sail offers from London to Dublin, via the Holyhead Ferry.)
