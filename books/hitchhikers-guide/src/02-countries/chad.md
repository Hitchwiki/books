# Chad

**Chad** is a country in the Sahel, south of [Libya](Libya "Libya"){.wikilink}, east of [Niger](Niger "Niger"){.wikilink} and [Cameroon](Cameroon "Cameroon"){.wikilink}, north of the [Central African Republic](Central_African_Republic "Central African Republic"){.wikilink}, and west of [Sudan](Sudan "Sudan"){.wikilink}. It shares a short border with [Nigeria](Nigeria "Nigeria"){.wikilink}.

## Borders

If you came over land via [Libya](Libya "Libya"){.wikilink}, [Niger](Niger "Niger"){.wikilink} or [Nigeria](Nigeria "Nigeria"){.wikilink} and you haven\'t been checked and did\'t get a stamp in your passport, register yourself in the next prefecture to avoid troubles. You need a travel permit for each prefecture you travel in and a visa before you enter the country.

## Dangers

There are landmines in the Tibesti mountains in the north of Chad. There is a high crime rate with road blocks, robbery and kidnapping. If you want to go hitchhiking in Chad, check out additional [safety](hitchhiker's_safety "safety"){.wikilink} measures. Be careful when you take photos, you need a permission to do this.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Chad against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on. If you can read the official text, please replace this paragraph with the provision it turns on and a link to it.
