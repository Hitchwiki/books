# Uruguay

The **Oriental Republic of Uruguay** is a country in [South America](South_America "South America"){.wikilink}.

More than half of Uruguay\'s population is concentrated in the capital of [Montevideo](Montevideo "Montevideo"){.wikilink}, meaning a very low population density in the country\'s interior.

## Hitchhiking

In the Uruguayan countryside you will doubtlessly encounter some of the most laid back, friendly folks you could imagine. Humble, generous and curious about anyone passing through, it is the people rather than the geography that make travel memorable in this overlooked corner of the continent. The tranquility of the countryside and the openness of the people will leave an impression on any traveler. Uruguay is a relatively small country, so traveling from the top Northern city to [Montevideo](Montevideo "Montevideo"){.wikilink} would not take much time. From [Rivera](Rivera "Rivera"){.wikilink} (a border town with [Brazil](Brazil "Brazil"){.wikilink}) to Montevideo it is about 520 km.

Even the [president is picking up hitchhikers](http://www.elpais.com.uy/informacion/hombre-hacia-dedo-nadie-paraba.html) in this country (January 2015).

Regarding hitchhiking and vagabonding, Uruguay\'s a pretty easy place to hang out. Many Uruguayan locals have hitchhiked through their country, and have had great experiences and good rides. Probably about 1 in 4 cars will give you a lift (outside of Montevideo). There are many stretches of desolate (but pretty) highway where you\'ll be lucky to see 4 cars pass in an hour. Expect long waits. Next to that, in the countryside much traffic just goes to the next farm or ranch, thus local traffic is prevalent and short rides more likely than somebody travelling longer distances. The coast, from [Chuy](Chuy "Chuy"){.wikilink} to [Colonia del Sacramento](Colonia_del_Sacramento "Colonia del Sacramento"){.wikilink}, is more trafficked and therefore an easier place to get a lift. Along the Interbalnearia some cool towns, sand dunes, and even sea lions can be seen.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

Uruguayan law does not mention hitchhiking, and it has no motorway category that shuts pedestrians out. [Article 22 of Ley 18.191](https://www.impo.com.uy/bases/leyes/18191-2007), the traffic and road safety act, says pedestrians must use the pavements and not the carriageway --- and then, at paragraph 3, that on public roads where there is no pavement they are to walk on the *bermas* or *banquinas*, the shoulders, **facing the oncoming traffic**. That is a positive permission to be on the shoulder of an inter-city route, which is exactly where hitchhiking happens. Paragraph 4 asks you to cross quickly and perpendicular to the axis, so treat crossing to the right side as the risk rather than standing. Nothing in the Act forbids asking a driver for a lift, and the *peajes* and *estaciones de servicio* on Rutas 1, 5, 8 and 9 are where drivers have already stopped.

## Sleeping out {#sleeping_out}

Montevideo is the only city in the country where it\'s not recommended to sleep on the street, in the rest of the country you can sleep rough without a care. In the countryside, when camping out or walking through fields, be aware of (toxic) snakes. Football stadiums are recommended. Another option in the countryside is to do a visit to the local police office. They most likely might be bored and in many cases invite you for dinner and to sleep in the station, or to put your tent nearby.

## Personal Experience {#personal_experience}

There is very few traffic on the Uruguayan highways, hence the fact that the entire country only has 3.5 million inhabitants. We could not hitch a single ride on that day, and so we called it a night after it started getting dark. The next day we walked and hitched, and walked and hitched\...until a truck picked us up, and took us all the way to Montevideo. Which was some 490 kilometers from where we were standing. Hitchhiking in Uruguay is definitely better then Argentina.

Traffic can be very low at times, as described above. Hitchhiking around the Ciudad de la Costa east of (next to) Montevideo seems to be very difficult, several times failed in hitchhiking between Carrasco airport and Atlantida. All other roads are fine, just be aware that anywhere outside of the cities traffic gets rare fastly, mostly locals driving to the next estancia. When waiting to get a straight ride to a next, bigger city, e.g. between Melo and Tacuarembo, one can wait ages. Hopping between estancias works fine, but even seemingly short-distance trips in the countryside can take their time. Notable, though, is that [night hitchhiking](night_hitchhiking "night hitchhiking"){.wikilink} works pretty awesome so far, from Paysandu to Montevideo it was no problem at all, and also from Rocha to Maldonado once it took me less than a couple of minutes to score a ride (given that traffic exists at that time of day).
