# Kiribati

**Kiribati** is an archipelago nation in [Micronesia](Micronesia "Micronesia"){.wikilink}, [Oceania](Oceania "Oceania"){.wikilink}.

## Hitchhiking

Hitchhikernick - visited in early october 2023 for 5 days. Transportation between pacific islands was via united and nauru airlines. Walking between islands was easy when tide was out and that is how i crossed between the islands of tarawa. Once on north island, i hitchhiked motorbikes, easily, all the way to the northern tip (Naa) where i camped (very hot and dripping sweat in the tent, though cooled down through the night). I am not sure if you need special permission to visit the north tarawa island, but the teacher, with her young students hinted that i did, though let me continue onwards with no issues. I slept on beach on the north east side (Betio), for 2 nights, of the main island of tarawa, but it made the base of my tent smell very strongly of urine due to the pollution. I spent the last night at a church, where i had free food, free laundry, a shower, fanned room with bed, and free food, all thanks to the tourist office (Bonriki) when asking them about camp sites around main town. The church minister then drove me to airport the next morning, buying me a meal, several drinks and givnig me some money 😁. No crazy dogs on this island. Bumped into two youngbuck american missionaries, before going to tourist info office, that i never thought i would see in a place like this.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Kiribati against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on.
