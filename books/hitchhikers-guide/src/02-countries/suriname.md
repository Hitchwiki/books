# Suriname

**Suriname** is a country in [South America](South_America "South America"){.wikilink}. Since it\'s a former colony of the [Netherlands](Netherlands "Netherlands"){.wikilink}, Dutch is the official language.

## Access

From Europe you will have to fly from [Schiphol](Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol "Schiphol"){.wikilink} airport ([Amsterdam](Amsterdam "Amsterdam"){.wikilink}); there is also flight coming from [Miami](Miami "Miami"){.wikilink}. By land you can come from [French Guiana](French_Guiana "French Guiana"){.wikilink} (part of [France](France "France"){.wikilink}) or [Guyana](Guyana "Guyana"){.wikilink}. In both cases there is a river to cross, the French side has a bridge, the British side doesn\'t and to cross you have to chose between *the big boat* (the official, legal ferry, this is for people with passports) or *the little boats* (home-owned, illegal boats that take locals back and forth across the border; only use this option if you don\'t have the correct paperwork and remember you will be a legal alien once on the other side\...)

## Visas

As of 2025, Suriname is the only country in South America that requires a Visa/Entry fee voucher for Europeans to enter the country. The cost in 2025 was 52US\$ or 52euros. Application can be made online via the Vfsglobal website. Alternatively, if you are coming from French Guiana, (or are having problems with the online application), you can get it from the Surinamese consulate in the border town of San Laurent-du-Maroni. (Card only, no cash accepted). Customs declarations using paper forms on arrival were officially phased out on Jan 15th 2025 and now have to be completed online at most 3 days before arrival and departure.

## Road network {#road_network}

The road network is concentrated on the coast. Thus most hitchhiking happens East to West, although the dirt tracks in the interior of the country can also be hiked on. In that case you will travel with miners and loggers going down into the forest for the job.

## Boat and Plane Hitchhiking {#boat_and_plane_hitchhiking}

Suriname is organized largely around its waterways, some villages just a few kilometers away from the capital are only accessible by boat, and the taxi-like motorized boats are everywhere on the rivers. It may be possible to get a lift in a boat, perhaps by offering to help with the landing procedure (although you would have to know how to line-handle at least a little), or by making friends with a boat owner. Otherwise be aware most people do pay to travel by boat.

Not many sailing boats come to Suriname, although it is technically the landing point if you want to make the shortest crossing possible of the atlantic from the [Cape Verde](Cape_Verde "Cape Verde"){.wikilink} islands. There is a place called Domburg, on the west coast of the Suriname river, just a half hour down south of Paramaribo, where a private owner lets out a few moorings and some yachts have even had their owners living permanently for about 3 or 4 years (in 2011). The people who do own yachts and come to Suriname will most probably come to Domburg at some point in their visit, and they will almost always be Dutch-speakers (it\'s the number one reason that people come to Suriname because it is the sole Dutch-speaking country on the continent!). If you are lucky enough and have patience, you may find somebody there who would take you on their boat, and their destination from there in 99% of the cases will be [Trinidad](Trinidad "Trinidad"){.wikilink}.

If also seems possible to travel inland by hitchhiking a small propeller plane like a small Cesna or Piper machine, which go to bumpy airfields somewhere in the jungle.

## Legality of Hitchhiking {#legality_of_hitchhiking}

*The law here has not been checked.* Nobody has yet read the road traffic rules in force in Suriname against the official text, so this section cannot tell you what they say, and nothing elsewhere on this page should be read as a statement of the law. What holds almost everywhere is that hitchhiking is not an offence in itself and that what the traffic code regulates is where a pedestrian may stand --- off the carriageway, and off motorway-class roads and their slip roads altogether --- but whether that is true here is simply unknown. Ask locally before standing on a major road, and treat the rest of this article as what hitchhikers report rather than as a rule you can rely on. Visited for three days on a transit visa coming from Guyana going to French Guiana in June 2024.  I struggled to get a lift from the ferry crossing until i went to where the snack shop is and the guard there bought me some beers and gave me a lift, just south of Nickerie.  From there i had to walk a while until i got to edge of town where I camped and got a lift the next morning. From there I got mixture of cars and buses to Paramaribo. From there I got a bus out to Tamanredjo where i walked for a while then set up camp in abondoned house (boy oh boy did it rain that night). from there it took 2 rides to the border town, where i was inundated by boat owners to take me to french guiana, but i just went to immigration office and waited until other passengers turned up and shared boat with them for 5€pp.  it proved almost impossible to get lifts at night for me.  Average waiting time 2.5 hrs. Two foreigners obtained visas at border (30day tourist for 50usd) and immigration officer asked if i required one, but got mine online with VFS global beforehand.
