# Helsinki

> Named dumpsters and map pins go stale. For current spots use [dumpstermap.org](https://dumpstermap.org/) and the live Trashwiki page. This chapter is culture, legality, and general practice — not a dumpster list.

**Helsinki**, the capital of Finland, offers many opportunities for freegans. [Stading Aikapankki](http://stadinaikapankki.wordpress.com/) is a Community Exchange Timebank where you can exchange services with local currency Tovi. [Kallio Sharetribe](https://kallio.sharetribe.com/en/) and [KuinOma](http://www.kuinoma.fi/) are examples of services that help you exchange, loan out and give away stuff. Dumpster-diving is a tad more difficult but there are accessible bins here and there. Besides, Finnish winters help to keep even [dairy](dairy "dairy"){.wikilink} and [meat](meat "meat"){.wikilink} produce freshly frozen.

## Markets

- The [Hakaniemen Kauppahalli (Hakaniemi)](http://www.hakaniemenkauppahalli.fi/) wastes food every day, although most stuff is thrown away on Saturdays as they are not open on Sundays. Especially the bakeries are worth asking. Be there at least half an hour before closing time. Mondays to Fridays it closes at 6 pm, Saturdays at 4 pm.

## Suppers together {#suppers_together}

- [Let\'s eat together](https://www.facebook.com/lets.eat.together) - Facebook group where you can just go to eat at someone\'s else place (Helsinki group, but everybody can open new groups in any city!)

## Discounted food {#discounted_food}

[Froodly](http://froodly.com/) it\'s an app that shows you where to get discounted food, for the moment it\'s just working in Helsinki and you can\'t get stuff for free, but with time it may be popular in other countries and could incentive Supermarkets to give away food nearly for free (hopefully even completely for free!)
