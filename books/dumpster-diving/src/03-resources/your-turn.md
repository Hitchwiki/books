# Your turn

You do not need a manifesto to look in a bin. You need a bag, a light, and the nerve to open a lid that other people walk past.

The practice pages exist so you do not make a mess, do not eat something you should not, and do not pick a fight with a law you have not read. The country and city pages exist so you are not shocked that a locked compactor in one place is an unlocked fruit crate in another. The resource pages exist so you can find the live map and the rest of the network. None of that replaces walking around the back of a shop after closing.

Start small. Bread and vegetables from a supermarket you already know. Take what you will eat this week. Close the bags. Go home and cook. If it felt ordinary, that is the point: a huge amount of food is ordinary until someone decides it is trash.

If you were waiting for permission, this is not quite that — dumpster diving sits in a legal grey area in many countries, and this book is not legal advice. It is a record of people who looked. You can look too. Read the local chapter. Then go see.
