# Recycling Guides

How can you [recycle](recycle "recycle"){.wikilink} what you find or already have?

Use your imagination and expand it! (up-cycle clothes, fix abandoned bikes or furniture, arts out of rubbish, \...)

Styrofoam (Expanded Polystyrene)

If you ship anything or have anything shipped to you, chances are, you end up with a surplus of expanded polystyrene. Recycling it can be much more cost-efficient and beneficial for the environment, but some recyclers prefer expanded polystyrene to be run through an [EPS Densifier](http://www.homeforfoam.com/city-governments/recycling-resources/recycling-equipment), which melts the foam down to take up less room. Talk to your local waste experts about how they handle polystyrene. Mealworms can be used to consume polystyrene and make it biodegradable if you\'re truly dedicated.

Wood Pallets and Dunnage

Vinyl dunnage bags were designed to be reusable. As long as there's no contamination, the most cost-effective option is to put them back on the truck as many times as possible. Wood boards, planks and pallets can also be reused until they break. And even then, as long as they're free from nails and staples, wood items can be ground up for mulch.

White Goods

Appliances that contain Freon, like air conditioners, refrigerators and dehumidifiers can be recycled but are regulated in how they must be handled. Since the creation of the [Clean Air Act](https://www.epa.gov/laws-regulations/summary-clean-air-act), companies and individuals must find an EPA compliant recycler to remove Freon and document its removal. Failure to follow the EPA guidelines and provide proper documentation can result in your company being slapped with a fine. But once you have the Freon removed, metal and other materials in these appliances can be recycled and rebates can be earned. If you're recycling white goods in bulk, you may even be able to create a revenue stream.

Mattresses

Not surprisingly, used mattresses are costly to haul and dispose of in a landfill. They are difficult to move and take up a lot of space. Businesses like hotels that often have to replace mattresses can save significantly on hauling costs and take advantage of landfill diversion rebates by recycling them for raw materials.
