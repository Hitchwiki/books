# Lisbon

> Named dumpsters and map pins go stale. For current spots use [dumpstermap.org](https://dumpstermap.org/) and the live Trashwiki page. This chapter is culture, legality, and general practice — not a dumpster list.

**Lisbon** is the capital and largest city of [Portugal](Portugal "Portugal"){.wikilink}.

Please keep in mind that there is loads of unemployement, poverty and homelessness in the city, and in many of these places there is other people eager for the leftovers. Said that, many, many dumpsters are left untouched, with good food getting wasted every day.

## [Food markets](Food_markets "Food markets"){.wikilink} {#food_markets}

In Lisbon, like in most towns in Portugal, there are many [vegetable markets](vegetable_market "vegetable market"){.wikilink}, where small vendors sell fruits and veggies that come directly from producers. They are usually open every day, until 1pm or 2pm.

It\'s good to pass some minutes before the closing stress, and ask vendors for things they won\'t sell (although some will tend to give you stuff that they are not even throwing away, out of charity). Good spots are Mercado da Ribeira, in Cais do Sodré, Mercado de Arroios, close to Arroios metro station, or Mercado de Benfica.

Here is a [http://www.cm-lisboa.pt/viver/comercio/mercados list](http://www.cm-lisboa.pt/viver/comercio/mercados_list "http://www.cm-lisboa.pt/viver/comercio/mercados list"){.wikilink} of all markets in Lisbon, with opening hours (in portuguese).

Useful phrases (basic Portuguese would be useful):

- \"Something to throw away\" = *\"algo para deitar fora?\"* or *\"algo para o lixo?\"* (*\"alg prolish\"* \[rough pronunciation\])
- \"Do you have fruits or veggies you are not selling anymore?\" = *\"Tem frutas ou legumes que já não vai vender?\"*

## [Bakeries](Bakeries "Bakeries"){.wikilink}

\"Padaria Portuguesa\" is a recent bourgeouis bakery chain, which gives the leftovers for charity, but will still give you some (or loads, depending on the day) if you pass by at closing time. Each shop has different closing hours, but the most common one is 8 pm.

\"Eric Kayser\" is a very expensive bakery chain. In Rua dos Carmo you find one, and they throw their stuff in dumpsters placed in the stairway next to the shop. You will find bread and pastry. They close at 8.30 pm (to be confirmed)

Paul\'s bakery on Rua Augusta 142, throws out a huge sack of really good baguettes (not filled, but some with seeds and olives), possibly every day. Closes at 10, go around 10.45. They don\'t seem to mind people taking them.

## Other [freegan](freegan "freegan"){.wikilink} opportunities {#other_freegan_opportunities}

Portuguese are addicted to big shopping malls. They are everywhere and they are great places for table diving. Colombo (metro Colégio Militar), Vasco da Gama (metro/train Oriente), or Armazéns do Chiado (smaller one, but the only one in the centre), are good examples.

There are great \"table diving\" opportunities at the food court at the Oriente shopping center. Portuguese mostly have lunch at the same fixed time daily so just go then and there will be a lot of things for scavenging.

This is also true for the Pingo Doce cafes where left over food is often left on plates on trays in the racks for a while. `<small>`{=html}(Last checked: October 2010)`</small>`{=html}

## [Flea market](Flea_market "Flea market"){.wikilink} {#flea_market}

There is a flea market called \"Feira da Ladra\" every Tuesday and Saturday in Campo de Santa Clara, near the Santa Apolónia train and Metro station.
