# Strasbourg

**Strasbourg** is the capital and main city of **Alsace** region in north-eastern France. Located close to the border with Germany, it is the capital of the **Bas-Rhin** department. With 638,670 inhabitants in 2006, Strasbourg\'s metropolitan area is the 9th largest in France.

![](New_banniere_CS_Strasbourg_WIKI600.jpg){width="350"}

Strasbourg is the seat of several European institutions such as the **Council of Europe** (with its European Court of Human Rights, its European Directorate for the Quality of Medicines and its European Audiovisual Observatory) and the Eurocorps as well as the **European Parliament** and the European Ombudsman of the European Union. The city is the seat of the Central Commission for Navigation on the Rhine.

Strasbourg\'s historic city centre, the*Grande Île* (**\"Grand Island\"**), was classified a World Heritage site by UNESCO in 1988, the first time such an honor was placed on an entire city centre.

Strasbourg is fused into the Franco-German culture and although violently disputed throughout history, has been a bridge of unity between France and Germany for centuries, specially through the **University of Strasbourg**, currently the largest in France, and the co-existence of Catholic and Protestant culture.

Economically, Strasbourg is an important centre of manufacturing and engineering, as well as of **road, rail, and river communications**. The port of Strasbourg is the second largest on the Rhine after Duisburg, Germany. In terms of city rankings, Strasbourg has been ranked 3rd in France and 18th globally for innovation. The city has been largely omitted from top rankings based on quality of living.

You can find more information about [Strasbourg on this article](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) on WikiPedia (or [here](http://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) in french).

## History of Strasbourg {#history_of_strasbourg}

Strasbourg was once **german**, then (1681) **french**, then (1871) **german**, then (1919) **french**, then **german again** during World War II, then eventually **french**.

The city reflects the inheritage of both cultures.

More informations on [http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg#History Wikipedia](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg#History_Wikipedia "http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg#History Wikipedia"){.wikilink}!

## How to come to Strasbourg {#how_to_come_to_strasbourg}

### By train {#by_train}

Strasbourg Central Station is located not far from the town center (10 minutes walk or two tram stops). Tickets may be purchased from the [SNCF website](http://www.voyages-sncf.com).

The closest [german railway (DB)](http://www.bahn.de) station is Kehl Bahnhof, only 5 km away from Strasbourg center. You may easily reach it by train (most train stopping at Kehl will go further to Strasbourg), car, bicycle or with the bus n°21. A good tip to reach Strasbourg from Germany is to buy a [Schönes Wochenende Ticket](http://www.bahn.de/i/view/USA/en/prices/germany/happy_weekend_ticket.shtml).

### By plane {#by_plane}

[Strasbourg Entzheim Airport](http://www.strasbourg.aeroport.fr/index.php) may be reached by regional train in only 9 minutes. There are flights from [Air France](http://www.airfrance.fr).

The following airports may be reached from Strasbourg railway station with shuttles from different travel companies ([Strasbourg navette](http://www.strasbourg-navette.fr/), [Alsace navette](http://alsace-navette.com/aeroport/index.html) or [Lufthansa](http://www.lufthansa-airportbus.com/strecken_strasbourg.html)):

- [Baden airport FKB](http://www.badenairpark.de/sub_airport/index-en.html) is one hour away from Strasbourg. You may find flights from [Ryanair](http://www.ryanair.com/) or [Air Berlin](http://www.airberlin.com/).

- [Euro-airport Basel-Mulhouse BSL](http://www.euroairport.com/) is one hour and a half away. You can fly with [Easyjet](http://www.easyjet.com/) or [Swiss](http://www.swiss.com/).

- [Frankfurt Hahn HHN](http://www.hahn-airport.de/) is 2 hours and 45 minutes away. There you\'ll find [Ryanair](http://www.ryanair.com/).

### By car or carsharing {#by_car_or_carsharing}

There\'s a couchsurfing.org/group.html?gid=2891 CS group for **carsharing** from/to Strasbourg. You can also try this website: [Covoiturage.fr](http://www.covoiturage.fr/)

A big part of Strasbourg downtown is reserved for pedestrians; streets are narrow, often full of tourists, and **all parking places require a payment**, else you could be fined. Parking is free on sunday and from 7PM to 8AM but finding a place is truly a challenge!

If you wish to reach the center just for a few hours, you can find a [public parking](http://www.strasbourg.eu/deplacement/informations/Plan_tarifs_voirie.jpg?FileID=documentsprincipaux%2fse_deplacer%2fstationnement_residant%2fplan_tarifs_voirie.jpg). The closest they are to the center, to more expensive they are.

Our advice is that you should let you car outside the town and get in a tram. Some parkings located near a tram stop are called \"**P+R**\" for [\"Parking and Relay\"](http://www.cts-strasbourg.fr/Tarifs/Tickets/tabid/595/language/fr-FR/Default.aspx#p_r). There you can park your vehicule and get a return ticket for every passenger up to 7 people for **2,90 €** (except the closest one, *Rotonde*, costing 3,60 €). P+R are open from Monday to Saturday from 7AM to 8PM but don\'t worry, if you come back later, you can always get out of the parking.

### By bus {#by_bus}

There\'s a [Eurolines](http://www.eurolines.fr/FRA/destinations/reseau-eurolines/) bus stop at the Place de l\'Étoile with connections to the whole Europe. From Romania, you may also use [Atlassib](http://www.atlassib.fr/csp/webatlassfr/index.csp).

### By bike {#by_bike}

Strasbourg is at the crossing of three main european cycling route: the Euroveloroute n°5, from London to Italy through Brussels and Luxembourg, the wonderful [EVR n°6](http://www.eurovelo6.org/front-page?set_language=en), from Nantes to Budapest and the [Rheinradweg](http://www.rheinradweg.eu/en/), from Rotterdam to Swiss.

## Get around Strasbourg {#get_around_strasbourg}

![Strasbourg Tram Transportation Plan](Réseau_Tram_Strasbourg_2010.jpg "Strasbourg Tram Transportation Plan"){width="250"}

### By foot {#by_foot}

The center of the town is located in the inner island, but the railroad station neighborhood and the Krutenau area are also very popular places to be. If you couchsurf in the center, or railroad station area, or Krutenau, you won\'t need any transportation.

### Bus and tramway {#bus_and_tramway}

Strasbourg has a **nice and complete public transportation network** working up to 0PM30 with buses and tramways. In the evening, you\'ll find a tram every 15 minutes and during the week-end, a [special night bus](http://www.cts-strasbourg.fr/Sed%C3%A9placer/R%C3%A9seaubusettram/Lignedenuit/tabid/214/language/fr-FR/Default.aspx) drives through the whole city from 11PM30 to 5AM30.

You\'ll find a [detailled transportation map here](http://www.cts-strasbourg.fr).

A ticket costs **1,40 €** and is valid one hour in both tramways and urban buses. A day tickets costs 4 € and a day ticket for up to 3 people costs 5,50 €.

All detailled prices are on the [Strasbourg Transportation Company (CTS) website](http://www.cts-strasbourg.fr/Tarifs/Tickets/tabid/595/language/fr-FR/Default.aspx).

### By bicycle {#by_bicycle}

Strasbourg town center isn\'t that big. However, a [bike rent](http://www.velhop.strasbourg.eu/) might be a good idea when it comes to going back home late or getting fast from one point to another. Some independent bike shops may also propose bike rental.

Over 3 or 6 months, buying a bike may be a cheapest option. You may check the couchsurfing.org/group.html?gid=19164 \"Jobs and services\" CS forum to see if someone\'s got one available, or try at [L\'affaire à faire](http://affaire-a-faire.creation-website.com/).

## What to see {#what_to_see}

As in every town, there are in Strasbourg a few unavoidable spots and many hidden things to discover. Most of the city center is reserved for pedestrians, so go for a walk in the old center, keep your eyes in the air and enjoy the architecture!

### The essential {#the_essential}

- [**Notre-Dame de Strasbourg**](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg_Cathedral) is a both Roman and Gothic Cathedral and one of the main symbols of Strasbourg.

![Strasbourg Cathedral](Cathedrale_Strasbourg_02.JPG "Strasbourg Cathedral"){width="110"} At 142 metres, it was the world\'s tallest building from 1647 to 1874, when it was surpassed by St. Nikolai\'s Church in Hamburg. Today, it\'s the sixth-tallest church in the world. Inside, you can see an 18-metre astronomical clock with animated characters launch into movement at different hours of the day. You can also climb up to the plateform at 66 meters (4,6 € full price, 2,3 € for children under 18 and students, free on the first sunday of the month) from where you have a beautiful view on Strasbourg, the \"Plaine d\'Alsace\", the Vosges and Black Forest.![La Petite France](Petite_France_Strasbourg.jpg "La Petite France"){width="300"}More info about the cathedral and visits: [Strasbourg Cathedral Website](http://www.cathedrale-strasbourg.fr/horloge.aspx)

- [**La Petite France**](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petite-France), a lovely quarter of the city with beautiful old and traditional alsatian houses, typical restaurants, \"Souvenirs\" shops\... As the quarter is crossed by several channels, watching boats crossing locks or moving along the water is a peaceful and every moment entertainment.

![European Institution](European_Institution_Strasbourg.jpg "European Institution"){width="220"}

- [**The European institutions**](http://www.strasbourg-europe.com/fr/institutions/) are a bit far from the city center. The European Parliament ad the European Court of Human Rights can be easily reached with the tramway, but as they usually can\'t be visited, a simple sight from a boat tour may be a sufficient option.

### The churches {#the_churches}

- **Protestant Church of St Pierre le Jeune**, rue de la Nuée Bleue. The church is fully painted inside and has a beautiful and recently restored convent. A very quiet, secret and beautiful place.
- **Saint Thomas Church**, with sculptures from Pigalle, Silbermann organ. It\'s on the way to the Petite France, and worth a look.
- **Saint Guillaume Church** (bell tower and Silbermann organ)
- **Saint Paul Church** (two stone bell towers)
- **Saint Nicolas Church**

- The **sculpted doors** in the Rue de l\'Epine,
- The **botanical garden**,
- The **Lycée des Pontonniers**, with its fantastic architecture, rue des Pontonniers,

- The **Civil Hospital** (Place de l\'Hopital), with its 1537 pharmacy and [Wine cellar](http://www.vins-des-hospices-de-strasbourg.fr/), which owns the oldest wine in the world, as old as 1472. You can visit the cellar and buy quality wines.
- After spending the whole day touring, go for a walk along the Ill river, sit on a bench and drink a beer!

## Museums

In Strasbourg, you\'ll find town and university museums, those ones beeing more science-oriented. **Public museums are free on the 1st Sunday of the month!**

### Town museums {#town_museums}

Town museums are closed on tuesday, except the Modern Art Museum, closed on Monday.

- The [Musée Alsacien](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-alsacien-en) presents folkloric stuffs, costumes and popular objects in a nice ensemble of **old alsatian houses**.
- The [Musée Archéologique](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-archeologique-en), in the Rohan Palace\'s basement, presents the oldest details concerning Alsatian history, from Prehistory (-600,000 years) to the dawn of the Middle Ages (800A.D.).
- Museum of Modern art ([MAMCS](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=mamcs-en))
- [Musée des Arts décoratifs](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-des-arts-decoratifs-en), also in the Rohan\'s palace, is divided into two sections: on one hand the sumptuous apartments of the Cardinals of Rohan and on the other hand the collection of Decorative Arts covering the period from 1681 to the mid-19th century featuring ceramics, furnishings, sculpture and paintings, metalwork and goldsmith\'s art. The museum is also showing a selection of mechanical toys from the Tomi Ungerer Foundation.
- [Musée des Beaux-Arts](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=Musee-des-Beaux-Arts-en) present a selection of European paintings from its debut to 1870. The museum\'s reserves are open to the public every 1stSaturday of the month.
- [Cabinet des Estampes et des Dessins](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=Cabinet-des-Estampes-en) (print room), only open on demand.
- The [Musée Historique](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-histo-en) is located in the city\'s old slaughterhouse. Its intent is to evoke the city\'s urban, as well as its political, economic, social and cultural history. Nice relief plan of the town, and you can try old helmets: get you camera ready for a nice profile pic!
- The [Musée de l\'Œuvre Notre-Dame](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-ond-en) presents the works of those who have been, for centuries, taking care of the fragile pink sandstone Cathedral.
- The [Musée Tomi Ungerer](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-tomi-ungerer-en) presents works from the famous alsatian illustrator.
- The [Aubette](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-aubette-en) dancing rooms are a cathedral of modern art, puting the public inside the painting rather than in front of it. The have been recently restored and are an absolute **must-see**! The entrance is free but the opening hours are a bit tricky: Thursdays and Saturdays from 2pm to 5pm.

### University museums {#university_museums}

On the [Unistra website](http://collections.u-strasbg.fr/), you\'ll find a complete information about the following museums:

- The [Musée Zoologique](http://www.musees-strasbourg.org/index.php?page=musee-zoo-en) is currently one of France\'s richest natural history museums. Stuffed animals and pinned insects. Very calm and nicely **old fashioned**!
- The [botanical garden](http://jardin-botanique.u-strasbg.fr/), with its exotic **tropical greenhouse**.
- The [planetarium](http://planetarium.u-strasbg.fr/) has a theatre that will show you the stars and planets. Magic and entertaining! You can also watch the observatory, the 3rd biggest in France, old instruments, and a fascinating film about the Cathedral astronomical clock, this film being much more interesting than the one showed every day at 12 in the cathedral. Some **night openings**, check [the website](http://planetarium.u-strasbg.fr/visites-nocturnes.html)!
- Also, a [mineralogy](http://mms.u-strasbg.fr/) and a [sismology](http://eost.u-strasbg.fr/musee/) museum. Some collections (anatomy, egyptology, gypsotheque\...) are only open for special occasion (*nuit des musées, journées du patrimoine*).

### Others

In the southern suburb of Strasbourg, you can visit the [Chocolate museum](http://www.musee-du-chocolat.com/), a bit expensive and commercial. For cocoa addicts!

In the north, the former [Jewish ritual bath](http://www.ville-bischheim.fr/musee-juif.php) of Bischheim is worth a look with its beautiful Renaissance spiral stairs. The museum is free and open on demand (ask at the library, they have the key). The way from Strasbourg is nice and may be a good occasion for a bike ride.

## CS Meetings {#cs_meetings}

Since September 2010, CS also gather on **Sunday nights** at the bar **The Irish**, to participate to a quizz. The quizz is in english and french, and includes 30 questions (general culture), 10 \"blind-test\", and 10 pictures. The best team wins a bottle, and the outsiders and best joke both win free shots.

There are also Regular CS Meeting every 1st and 16th of each monthS.

Check couchsurfing.org/group.html?gid=5615 Strasbourg CS forum to learn more about those meetings and to be sure something is happening on Wednesday or Sunday nights.

Some CS from Strasbourg usually go to the bar **La Lanterne** on **Wednesday evenings**, as it is Happy Hours all night long. It used to be a regular meeting but not anymore. Anywau, You can still meet a lot of Cs there\... La Lanterne is a micro-brewery, so they brew their own beers: blond, amber and white beers. On Wednesday, you will get a normal 25 cl beer for 1,60 € and a 50 cl for 3,20 €. You can also get some local dishes: tarte flambées and knacks. People go there around 8\~8.30 and generally till the bar closes, which means 1.30 am.
