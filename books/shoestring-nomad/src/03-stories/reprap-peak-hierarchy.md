# RepRap @ Peak Hierarchy

[img\_assist|nid=545|title=BirdHouse|desc=A birdhouse made out of bio-plastic soon coming to the Casa?|link=none|align=right|width=200|height=150] Yesterday, some of us attended [a reading of our friend Michel Bauwens](http://freeknowledge.eu/events/lecture_P2P_Social_Production_Michel_Bauwens) and got the opportunity to meet [Erik](http://blog.erikdebruijn.nl), who opened our eyes of how easy it is to create our own customizable manufacturing capability.
Erik is involved in the [Fablab-movement](http://sharewiki.org/en/Fab_Labs) and he spoke with us about his experience with [RepRap](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RepRap_Project)'s. These are [3D printers](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printings) designed to be freely reproduced by anyone. The cost of making them yourself is 500 euro.
But here comes the funny thing: you don't actually have to buy these components to build up the printer, but you manufacture them through a different 3D-printer, by using *[shared digital designs](http://www.thingiverse.com/)*, and then assemble them yourself. 
So -just to repeat it to make sure you get it- you can use another 3D printer with free-source designs to build the components which you can then assemble into yet a new printer, that you can then use to produce yet another.
It is like making babies, but then without the sex :) Or for the scientists among us: it is a clone-machine. Or in yet other technical terms, the RepRap is largely a self-replicating machine that can be used for rapid-prototyping and manufacturing.
This [article](http://blog.erikdebruijn.nl/archives/70-The-personal-fabrication-revolution-Status-Quo-Ante.html) and this [video](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g0m1cVqNsRA) about a commercial printer will give you a good idea what this revolutionary free-source product is about.
The raw material that the printer uses is plastic - but one can also recycle used plastic or create [biodegradable plastics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioplastic) with ingredients such as potato starch - which is called [polylactic acid bio-plastics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polylactic_acid). There is also an article on [Instructables](http://www.instructables.com/id/Make-Potato-Plastic!) on how to build these bio-plastics.
